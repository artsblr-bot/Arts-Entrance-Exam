# Gurukul Arts — Recruitment MCQ Exam
### 32 Questions | Google Form Ready | For Camera, Gimbal, Editing & Content Team Applicants

**Purpose:** This exam is not looking for experts. It is looking for people who are **honest, curious, quick to learn, and trustworthy with expensive gear and premium software.** A candidate who knows less but answers with the right instincts is more valuable than one who knows more but shows the wrong instincts.

**Instructions for candidates (put this at the top of your Google Form):**
> "Answer every question honestly. There is no benefit in guessing what you think we want to hear — this exam is designed to catch that too. Pick the answer that is closest to what you would *actually* do."

## Scoring & Interpretation Guide

| Section | Marks | What it reveals |
|---|---|---|
| Photography (Q1-7) | 7 | Existing baseline knowledge + how they think about visuals |
| Videography (Q8-14) | 7 | Existing baseline knowledge + practical instincts |
| Editing/Software (Q15-21) | 7 | Familiarity with tools + problem-solving habits |
| Common Sense (Q22-27) | 6 | Real-world reaction quality, communication, composure |
| Integrity & Growth (Q28-32) | 5 | **The most important section** — trustworthiness with resources, honesty, and learning attitude |

**Total: 32 marks**

**How to read the results — not just the score:**
- A candidate scoring **low on technical sections (1-4) but 5/5 on Section 5** is often a better long-term hire than someone who scores high technically but weak on Section 5. Skill can be taught in weeks. Integrity and hunger to learn usually can't be.
- Watch for candidates who get **every single question "correct"** — it can mean genuine understanding, or it can mean they're answering what sounds right rather than what they'd actually do. Consider a short follow-up conversation with top scorers before finalizing.
- Use wrong answers in Sections 1-4 to spot **genuine interest**: someone who gets photography wrong but videography right, and seems energized talking about videography, likely belongs on video-focused work — not forced into photography.
